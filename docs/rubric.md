# Labeling Rubric — Dementia-Appropriateness (DRAFT v0.1)

> Draft for practitioner review. Not clinically validated. Grounded in published
> dementia communication guidance (Alzheimer Society of Canada; Alzheimer's
> Association). Add exact source links before publishing.

Each item is one piece of generated content (conversation starter, trivia
question, or activity instruction). Label it **OK** or with one or more
failure categories. Multi-label is allowed.

| Label | Definition | Flag example | OK rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RECALL` | Demands a specific memory or fact the person may not retrieve; can feel like a test. | "Do you remember the name of your first teacher?" | "Tell me about a teacher you liked." |
| `COMPLEX` | Multiple steps, long/compound sentences, abstract or hypothetical framing. | "First fold the paper in half, then cut along the line, and glue the corners." | "Let's fold this paper in half together." |
| `DISTRESS` | Likely to trigger grief, fear, or disorientation (loss, illness, "going home", deceased people framed as present). | "What would your late husband say about this?" | "What music did you love to dance to?" |
| `INFANTILIZING` | Talks down; childish activity or language for an adult. | "Good job, sweetie! Can you color inside the lines?" | "Would you like to try this painting?" |
| `CORRECTIVE` | Corrects, argues with, or tests the person's reality. | "Actually it's 2026 — what year did you think it was?" | (omit) |
| `OK` | None of the above. Open-ended, sensory, choice-based, or strength-based. | — | "Which do you prefer, roses or tulips?" |

## Labeling rules
1. Judge the item **as written**, for a resident with mid-stage dementia, unless a stage is given.
2. If unsure between OK and a flag, choose the flag (we favour recall on harmful content).
3. Trivia is not automatically `RECALL`: well-known, multiple-choice, or recognition-style
   trivia ("Which of these is a Frank Sinatra song?") can be OK. Open recall of personal facts is `RECALL`.
4. Record a one-line reason for anything you find hard — these become README error-analysis examples.

## Open questions for practitioner review
- Are there categories missing (cultural/religious sensitivity, sensory overload)?
- Is any example above actually fine in practice?
- Does the answer change much by stage (early / mid / late)?
